Mountain safety

The habits that keep good days good: right-of-way rules, helmet use, cold and altitude, and what every skier should know before leaving the marked runs.

The skier responsibility basics

Every country's code is worded differently, but they share the same core rules:

  1. Always stay in control and be able to stop or avoid people and objects.
  2. People ahead of you (downhill) have the right of way.
  3. Don't stop where you obstruct a trail or aren't visible from above.
  4. Look uphill and yield when starting downhill or merging.
  5. Use devices to prevent runaway equipment.
  6. Observe all signs and warnings; keep off closed trails.
  7. Know how to load, ride and unload lifts safely before using them.
  8. Don't ski or ride when impaired by alcohol or drugs.

Helmets

Helmets are standard on the slopes today and mandatory for children in several countries and resorts. Replace a helmet after any significant impact, even if no damage is visible.

Leaving the marked runs

Off-piste and backcountry terrain is not controlled or patrolled for avalanches. Before going there, every member of the group needs a transceiver (beacon), probe and shovel, the training to use them, and the current avalanche forecast. The safest approach is to hire a certified mountain guide.

Official avalanche forecasts: In North America see avalanche.org (US) and avalanche.ca (Canada). In Europe see avalanches.org (EAWS). Local forecasts take priority over any general guidance.

Cold, sun and altitude

  • Frostbite: watch for white or waxy skin on cheeks, nose and ears; get indoors and rewarm gradually.
  • Sun: UV is stronger at altitude and doubled by snow reflection. Use SPF 50 and good lenses.
  • Altitude: at resorts above about 2,500 m, some visitors experience headaches, poor sleep or nausea. Hydrate, limit alcohol on day one and descend if symptoms worsen. Seek medical advice for persistent symptoms.
This page is general information, not professional training or medical advice. Take a course, follow resort and patrol instructions, and consult a medical professional where needed.
