Ski trail ratings around the world

Why a blue in Colorado isn't a blue in Austria, and how to read a new trail map safely.

By eSKI Editorial · Updated November 2026 · Editorial policy

Run ratings are relative to each resort. There's no global standard for slope angle, so the same colour can mean different things in different places. Here's how the main systems work.

North America

  • Green circle — easiest; gentle, wide, groomed.
  • Blue square — intermediate; the bulk of most mountains.
  • Black diamond — advanced; steep, may be ungroomed or mogulled.
  • Double black diamond — expert; very steep, cliffs, chutes or exposure.
  • Orange ovals or other symbols often mark terrain parks.

Europe

  • Green (used in France, Spain and some others) — very easy.
  • Blue — easy; roughly comparable to many North American greens and easy blues.
  • Red — intermediate; can be steeper and narrower than North American blues.
  • Black — difficult.
  • Ski routes / itineraries (yellow or orange, dashed) — marked but often not groomed or patrolled like pistes; treat as off-piste-like.

Japan

Japanese resorts typically use green (beginner), red (intermediate) and black (advanced). Off-piste rules vary: many resorts prohibit leaving marked runs except through designated gates.

Using ratings safely

  1. Start your first run at a new resort one level easier than your usual.
  2. Ratings reflect the hardest section of a run.
  3. Conditions change difficulty: ice, flat light and fresh snow can make an easy run hard.
  4. Ask the lift staff or patrol if unsure.
