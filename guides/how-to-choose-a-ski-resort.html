How to choose the right ski resort

Seven questions that narrow thousands of resorts to the right three for your group.

By eSKI Editorial · Updated November 2026 · Editorial policy

The "best" ski resort doesn't exist. The best resort for your group, dates and budget does — and you can find it by answering seven questions in order. Each one eliminates options, so by the end you're comparing a short, realistic list.

1. Who is the weakest skier in the group?

Plan around the least experienced person, not the strongest. A resort with excellent beginner areas, a good ski school and plenty of gentle blue runs keeps everyone happy. Experts can always find challenge on a varied mountain; beginners can't conjure up gentle terrain on a steep one. Look for dedicated learning zones, conveyor lifts and a high proportion of easier runs.

2. How important is guaranteed snow?

Altitude and latitude are the biggest predictors of snow reliability. High-base resorts in the Alps (bases above roughly 1,800 m), the interior ranges of North America and Japan's Sea-of-Japan side are the safest bets. If you're booking far ahead for early December or late April, prioritise high, glacier-backed or north-facing terrain and strong snowmaking. Use the Powder Radar when booking short-notice.

3. What's the real budget — per person, all-in?

Lift access, lodging and travel dominate cost. North American destination resorts often carry the highest day-ticket prices, while many European, Andorran and Japanese resorts offer cheaper multi-day passes. Run your numbers in the trip cost calculator before falling in love with a resort.

4. How much travel time will you accept?

Count door-to-door time, including transfers. A resort 45 minutes from a major airport gives you an extra ski day on a short trip compared with one four hours away. For weekends, the closest decent mountain almost always beats the famous one.

5. What terrain do you actually want?

Groomed cruisers, trees, bowls, terrain parks, off-piste, or huge linked areas for touring from village to village? Europe's linked domains suit people who love covering distance; North American resorts tend to offer more tree skiing and inbounds avalanche-controlled steeps.

6. What happens off the slopes?

Non-skiers, young kids, nightlife lovers and foodies all need different villages. Ski-in/ski-out convenience, childcare, spas, restaurants and après culture can matter as much as the vertical drop.

7. Which pass do you already hold — or should you?

If you already own a multi-resort pass, it usually makes sense to choose a resort it covers. If you plan five or more days, check whether buying a pass is cheaper than day tickets. See our multi-resort pass guide.

FAQ

What's the best ski resort for beginners?

One with a dedicated learning area, conveyor lifts, a good ski school and many gentle runs. Large resorts with village-level beginner zones are ideal.

When is snow most reliable?

In the Northern Hemisphere, roughly mid-January to mid-March; in the Southern Hemisphere, mid-July to late August.
