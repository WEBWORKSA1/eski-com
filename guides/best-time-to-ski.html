The best time to ski, month by month

Balance snow quality, crowds and price with this region-by-region calendar.

By eSKI Editorial · Updated November 2026 · Editorial policy

Snow, crowds and price rarely peak together. Here's how the season usually unfolds; actual conditions vary year to year, so check the Powder Radar before you go.

Northern Hemisphere

  • November — high-altitude and glacier resorts open; limited terrain elsewhere. Good deals, unreliable coverage.
  • December — coverage builds through the month. Christmas–New Year is peak price and peak crowds.
  • January — cold, often excellent snow, quieter after the first week. Best value window; Japan's powder is usually at its deepest from mid-January.
  • February — deepest snowpack in many regions, but school holidays raise prices and crowds, especially in Europe.
  • March — longer, sunnier days, good base depth and spring storms. Excellent all-round month.
  • April — spring skiing: soft afternoons, closing parties, lower prices at high resorts.

Southern Hemisphere

  • June — openings in Australia, New Zealand, Chile and Argentina, snow permitting.
  • July–August — peak season and the most reliable snow.
  • September–October — spring conditions and late-season deals.

Rule of thumb

For the best mix of snow, value and space: mid-January to early February (avoiding local holidays) or the second half of March.
