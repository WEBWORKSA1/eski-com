15 proven ways to save money on a ski trip

Skiing is expensive, but the gap between the smart price and the walk-up price is huge.

By eSKI Editorial · Updated November 2026 · Editorial policy

The same week on the same mountain can cost twice as much depending on when and how you book. These tactics are ordered roughly by how much they save.

Lift access

  1. Never buy walk-up tickets at major resorts. Advance online and multi-day tickets are routinely much cheaper than the ticket-window price.
  2. Do the pass math. If you'll ski five or more days at resorts on one multi-resort pass, the pass often pays for itself.
  3. Look for "kids ski free" policies and family bundles, common in Europe and at some North American resorts.
  4. Ski smaller, independent mountains for some days; they're often far cheaper and less crowded.

Timing

  1. Avoid holiday weeks: Christmas–New Year, school half-terms and national holidays drive prices to the peak.
  2. Go in January (after New Year) or late March. Snow is often good and prices dip.
  3. Book lodging early — or very late when a storm is forecast and operators fill gaps.

Lodging

  1. Stay in the valley and drive or bus up; savings can be large.
  2. Self-cater with a kitchen for breakfast and dinners.
  3. Share a chalet or condo as a group and split the cost.

Gear & extras

  1. Rent skis in town, not at the base, and reserve online.
  2. Own boots and goggles; they're the items worth buying first.
  3. Pack lunch — on-mountain food is priced for captive customers.
  4. Bundle lessons with tickets and rentals; beginner packages are the best value in skiing.
  5. Get packaged quotes. Specialists can combine flights, lodging and passes at rates you can't book individually. Get up to three free quotes.

FAQ

When is the cheapest time to ski?

Early December (snow permitting), mid-to-late January and late March to April, excluding holidays.

Is a season pass worth it for one trip?

Sometimes. For trips of five or more days at resorts on the same pass, it can be cheaper than buying day tickets; compare both totals.
