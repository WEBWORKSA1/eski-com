Rent or buy?

For your first few seasons, buy boots and rent skis. Boots are the single piece of equipment that most affects comfort and control, and a properly fitted pair (ideally from a specialist boot fitter) will make every rental ski feel better. Skis are bulky to travel with and technology changes quickly; demo rentals let you try different widths and shapes before committing.

Buying skis starts to make sense when you ski more than roughly 10–12 days a season, always ski the same region, or know exactly which width and style you want.

Choosing ski width

  • Under 85 mm underfoot: carving on groomed runs; quick edge-to-edge.
  • 85–100 mm: all-mountain; the best one-ski quiver for most people.
  • 100–115 mm: freeride and powder-leaning all-mountain.
  • 115 mm+: deep powder specialists.

Bindings and DIN settings

Binding release values (often called DIN) must be set by a qualified technician based on your weight, height, boot sole length, age and skier type. Never adjust them yourself from an online chart — incorrect settings are a leading cause of preventable knee injuries.

Layering that works

Use a moisture-wicking base (merino or synthetic), an insulating mid layer you can remove, and a waterproof, breathable shell. Avoid cotton anywhere next to your skin.
