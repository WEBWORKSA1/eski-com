Learn to ski (or snowboard)

A no-nonsense beginner's guide: what to expect on day one, whether to ski or ride, and the free video lessons worth watching before you go.

Your first day, step by step

  1. Book a lesson. A half- or full-day group lesson with a certified instructor is the fastest, safest way to start. Many resorts bundle a beginner lift ticket, rentals and lesson at a discount.
  2. Get fitted. Rental boots should be snug but not painful, with your heel locked down. Wear one pair of thin ski socks — not two thick ones.
  3. Learn on the flat. Walking, sidestepping and the wedge ("pizza") happen before you ever ride a lift.
  4. Ride the magic carpet. Beginner areas use conveyor lifts on very gentle slopes.
  5. Link wedge turns. Turning is how you control speed. By the end of day one most people link turns on the easiest green or blue run.
  6. Stop before you're exhausted. Most beginner injuries happen late in the day. Quit while it's fun.

Skiing or snowboarding?

Skiing is usually easier on day one: your legs work independently and getting on and off lifts is simpler. Snowboarding is harder for the first two or three days, then progress often accelerates. Neither is "better" — take the quiz below.
